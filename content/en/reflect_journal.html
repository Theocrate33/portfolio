Reflection Journal

This journal captures my reflections on professional development, spiritual growth, leadership experiences, and lessons learned throughout my journey in Information Technology. Each reflection represents meaningful insights gained through study, service, challenges, and personal growth.

IT Support Specialist • Systems Administrator • Full-Stack Web Developer

Learning Through Challenges

Throughout my studies in Information Technology, I have learned that growth often comes through challenges rather than convenience. Networking configurations, database projects, programming assignments, and systems administration labs frequently presented problems that could not be solved immediately. These experiences taught me perseverance, patience, and continuous learning.

The message "Come What May, and Love It" helped me view difficulties differently. Rather than seeing obstacles as setbacks, I began to see them as opportunities for growth. Many technical skills I possess today were developed while troubleshooting problems, researching solutions, and refusing to give up when progress seemed slow.

This principle has influenced both my spiritual and professional development. Technically, it has helped me become a more resourceful problem solver. Spiritually, it has strengthened my faith that growth often comes through effort, persistence, and trust in the Lord.

Agency, Responsibility, and Professional Growth

One lesson that has become increasingly important to me is the principle of agency. Doctrine and Covenants 58 teaches that individuals should be anxiously engaged in a good cause and act for themselves rather than waiting to be compelled.

In Information Technology, this principle applies directly to learning. No instructor can learn networking, cybersecurity, programming, or cloud computing on my behalf. I must choose to study, practice, improve, and take responsibility for my development.

This principle has also influenced how I approach leadership and teaching. Whether helping students understand technical concepts or supporting users with technology problems, I recognize the importance of initiative and personal responsibility. Agency encourages me to take ownership of my choices and continue progressing toward my professional goals.

Faith and Technical Problem Solving

Information Technology often requires solving complex and unfamiliar problems. There have been many situations where technical knowledge alone did not immediately produce an answer. During these moments, I learned the value of prayer, patience, and thoughtful reflection.

Faith does not replace technical preparation, but it strengthens my confidence and perspective while seeking solutions. Through study, persistence, and prayer, I have often found that answers become clearer and opportunities for growth emerge.

As I continue developing as a systems administrator and cybersecurity professional, I hope to combine technical competence with faith, wisdom, and ethical decision-making.

Using Technology to Serve Others

One of the most important insights from my educational journey is that technology is ultimately about people. Computers, networks, applications, and databases are valuable because they help individuals learn, communicate, solve problems, and improve their lives.

This understanding has influenced many of my projects. Building learning platforms, teaching students, repairing computers, and supporting users have shown me that technology creates its greatest value when it serves others.

As I continue my career, I want my technical skills to be a means of service. I hope to use technology responsibly, ethically, and compassionately to contribute positively to my community and to the organizations I support.

Maintaining Alignment Between Faith and Technology

As I continue my professional development, I recognize that there are warning signs that could cause my spiritual beliefs and professional practices to become misaligned.

Some of these warning signs include prioritizing personal recognition over service, becoming impatient when working with others, neglecting ethical responsibilities, focusing only on technical success while ignoring the needs of people, or allowing professional pressures to weaken spiritual habits such as prayer and scripture study.

To maintain alignment, I strive to regularly reflect on my actions, seek spiritual guidance through prayer, study gospel teachings, and remember that technology is ultimately about serving individuals and communities. These practices help me ensure that professional success remains consistent with the values of faith, integrity, accountability, and service.
